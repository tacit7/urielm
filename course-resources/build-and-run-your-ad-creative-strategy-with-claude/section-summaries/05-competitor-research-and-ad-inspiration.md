# Competitor Research and Ad Inspiration

Time range: 31:25-47:55

The section begins by choosing the strongest core avatar foundation from Claude's research output. The preferred persona is the "burned learner": someone who already tried to learn AI through a course, tutorial, or certificate, spent money or effort, and came away without a useful practical skill. The instructor explains how this frustration creates a strong advertising angle, especially when paired with a differentiated mechanism like live office hours and a risk reversal such as a free trial or refund window.

After saving and reviewing the avatar document, the session moves into ad ideation. The instructor identifies four major sources for creative ideas: competitor or adjacent-brand ads, trending organic content, customer feedback and surveys, and a brand's own winning-ad database. The live demonstration focuses on Facebook Ad Library, using Claude and the Meta MCP to find relevant advertisers, then manually reviewing ads from an adjacent education brand, Brilliant.org, sorted by impressions and longevity to identify formats worth adapting.

- Pick the avatar that is both emotionally specific and commercially usable; in this case, the "burned learner" is stronger than narrower or less advertisable segments.
- A past failed learning experience can become a persuasive ad angle when the new offer directly addresses what was missing before.
- Unique mechanisms, such as weekly expert office hours, help reframe a familiar course offer as meaningfully different.
- Risk reversal matters for skeptical buyers who fear wasting money on another ineffective learning product.
- Avatar documents should be saved into project context and reviewed carefully because they become the foundation for later creative strategy.
- Strong copy often comes from second-order benefits: not just learning AI tools, but feeling competent, finishing the course, or looking more capable at work.
- Sub-avatars sharpen broad positioning by adding a specific pain point, such as wanting a structured path instead of a scattered content library.
- Common ad-idea sources include competitor ads, adjacent-brand ads, organic trends, customer research, and internal winning-ad data.
- Facebook Ad Library is useful for inspiration because active ad volume, impression sorting, and long run times can signal what may be working.
- Good inspiration does not have to come from a direct competitor; adjacent brands with similar business models or offers can provide reusable creative patterns.
