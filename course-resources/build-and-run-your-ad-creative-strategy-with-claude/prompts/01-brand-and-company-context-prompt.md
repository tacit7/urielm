Brand and company context Prompt
You are helping me build a Brand and Company Context file. This file will live inside a Claude project and will be read every time I ask for help with advertising: ideating ads, writing scripts, building briefs, analyzing performance. Everything you produce for me later depends on getting this right, so accuracy matters more than speed.
Work in three phases. Do not skip ahead.
PHASE 1: RESEARCH
First, ask me for my company name and website URL. Ask for nothing else yet.
Once I give you those, research the brand yourself. Read the site, the product and pricing pages, any about page, and whatever else is publicly visible. Build a working draft of what you can determine on your own:
* What the company does and what category it competes in
* The products, what they cost, and how the offers are structured
* How the brand positions itself and who it appears to be for
* The tone and language the brand uses about itself
* Any proof visible on the site: customer counts, results, logos, press, testimonials
Then show me a short summary of what you found, no more than 200 words. Ask me to correct anything wrong before you continue. Anything you inferred rather than read directly, mark with [confirm] so I can catch it.
PHASE 2: INTERVIEW
Now ask me the following 8 questions. Ask them ONE AT A TIME and wait for my answer before moving to the next one. Do not batch them, do not number them out loud, and do not add extra questions beyond these 8. If I say skip, move on without pushing.
1. Which products or offers are you actively advertising right now, what do they cost, what action do you want people to take, and where does the ad send them? Tell me what is on that page and what the offer is once they land.
2. What actually makes your product work, and why is that different from the alternatives? I am asking about the mechanism, not the benefit.
3. What are the main reasons people do not buy, and what actually answers each one?
4. What proof can we use in ads? Numbers, customer counts, results, recognizable customers, press, awards, or testimonials you are happy to quote.
5. On messaging: what angles or phrases have worked well for you in advertising, what has fallen flat, and are there any words or claims you never want used?
6. What ad formats have worked for you historically, and what can you actually produce right now? For example founder on camera, UGC, product footage, static design, or a library of existing ads.
7. Who is this product NOT for, and are there any legal or compliance limits on what you can claim? Skip this if neither applies.
8. Are there any specific competitors you want me to focus on when we do competitor ad research later?
PHASE 3: BUILD THE FILE
Once I have answered, write the finished document using exactly the structure below. Use my language wherever I gave you specific phrasing. Do not invent proof, results, or claims that I did not give you. If a section is thin because I skipped a question, write "Not provided" rather than filling it with something plausible.
[Company Name] — Brand and Company Context
1. What we do
One paragraph. What the company is, what it sells, and to whom.
2. Category and positioning
What market this sits in, and how the brand is positioned within it.
3. Products and offers
For each product being advertised: what it is, what it costs, how the offer is structured, and whether it is currently a priority for advertising.
4. The mechanism
What makes the product work and why that is different from the alternatives. This is the part that separates our ads from a competitor's.
5. The conversion path
Where ads send people, what is on that page, what the offer is once they arrive, and what action counts as a conversion. Any ad we write has to keep the promise this page makes.
6. Proof we can use
Numbers, customer counts, results, recognizable customers, press, awards, testimonials. Quote testimonials verbatim. Mark anything that cannot be used in paid ads.
7. Objections and counters
Each reason people do not buy, paired with what actually answers it.
8. Messaging that works
Angles, phrases, and hooks that have performed. Include the specific wording where I gave it to you.
9. Messaging that does not work
Angles and phrases that have fallen flat, and what we learned from each.
10. Never say
Words, phrases, claims, and tonal moves that are off limits. Include compliance restrictions here if there are any.
11. Voice
How the brand sounds. Sentence rhythm, formality, humor, and anything structurally distinctive. Include two or three example sentences that sound unmistakably like us.
12. Who this is not for
Explicit exclusions, so we do not write to an audience we do not want.
13. Formats and production capability
Formats that have worked, and what we can realistically produce right now.
14. Competitors to watch
The brands we want monitored for ad research, and how we differ from each.
After you produce the document, add a short note at the end telling me:
   * Which sections are thin and worth strengthening later
   * What single piece of information would most improve this file if I went and found it
Keep the document tight. Long is not the same as useful. Every line in here gets read by you on every future request, so anything vague makes your later output vaguer.
