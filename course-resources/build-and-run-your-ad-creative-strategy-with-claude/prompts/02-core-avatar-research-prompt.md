Core avatar research prompt
You are doing market research for an advertising project. Your job is to find what real buyers say in public, quote it accurately, and cite where it came from. You are not writing marketing copy and you are not summarizing into professional language.
CONTEXT
[In one or two sentences: what my product is, and who I think buys it.]
WHAT I NEED YOU TO DO
Search public sources where people in this category talk about the problem unprompted. Prioritize places where they are talking to each other rather than to a brand: forums, review sites, comment sections, community threads, question sites, and complaint or cancellation discussions.
Search Reddit first, and search it hard. It is the single best source for this kind of research because people describe problems there in their own words, before any brand is involved, and they argue with each other, which surfaces the objections. Specifically:
   * Find the 3 to 6 subreddits where this buyer actually spends time. Include the ones about their job, hobby, or life stage, not only ones about the product category.
   * Search those subreddits for people describing the problem, asking for recommendations, complaining about a solution they tried, and posting an update after trying something.
   * Read the comments, not only the posts. The most useful lines are usually someone replying "same thing happened to me" and then explaining what happened.
   * Note where the same complaint or phrase shows up across several different subreddits. Repetition across communities is the strongest signal in this whole exercise.
   * Tell me which subreddits you used and roughly how active each one is.
Then search the other source types. Two are worth extra attention: three star reviews, because they explain rather than rant, and cancellation or "why I left" threads, because someone describing why they quit a solution is describing a real outcome.
Prioritize what people said over what analysts concluded. One person explaining why they gave up on a solution is worth more here than a market size report.
RULES
   1. Quote verbatim. Keep their spelling, their slang, their run-on sentences. Do not clean anything up. Do not translate it into professional phrasing.
   2. Cite every quote with a link and say what kind of source it is.
   3. Never invent a quote. If you cannot find a real one for a section, write "no strong sources found" and move on.
   4. Separate what people SAID from what you CONCLUDED. Label conclusions as yours.
   5. Prefer recent material, within the last two years, unless the problem is timeless.
   6. Do not average things out. Two people saying opposite things is a finding, not a problem to resolve.
RETURN IT IN THIS STRUCTURE
   1. What they say they want
Specific wants, each written as a sentence starting with "I want" or "I need." Under each one, one or two verbatim quotes with links. Aim for 6 to 10 wants. Rank them by how often the theme appeared.
   2. What they have been through
Two lists.
Situations: circumstances or events these people are in or have been through.
Tried and failed: solutions they tried and what happened as a result. Include the outcome every time, even if the outcome is that they are still looking.
Verbatim quotes and links under each.
   3. How they feel about it
For each recurring feeling: the word they actually use, and the specific thing that sets it off. Quotes and links. Do not list emotions on their own. An emotion with no trigger attached is useless here.
   4. What they do about it
Habits, workarounds, rituals, and routines they have built around the problem. Include anything they do repeatedly, including mental habits like checking, re-reading, or second-guessing. Quotes and links.
   5. Who they appear to be
Only demographics that are visible in the sources or logically implied by the situations above. Do not guess. If the sources do not show it, say so.
   6. Their language
25 to 40 verbatim lines, first person, unedited, each with a link. Include the phrases that repeat across different sources, the specific words they use for the problem, and the words they use for the solution. Also list any terms the industry uses that buyers clearly do not use.
   7. Where the objections are
The reasons people in this category say they did not buy, did not stick with something, or regret what they chose. Quotes and links.
   8. What I could not find
Which of the sections above came back thin, and what kind of source would fill the gap. Be honest here rather than padding a section to look complete.
Do not add a conclusion, a recommendation, or a summary at the end.
