# Why Creative Strategy Matters

Time range: 01:58-07:10

## Synopsis

Brooke opens this section by wrapping the session housekeeping and explaining how participants should use the chat. She notes that questions can be saved for the Q&A at the end, while other session-related comments can still be handled as they arise. After mentioning the closing survey, she hands the session over to Rishi, who introduces himself as the head of growth at The Rundown and frames the webinar as a practical session on building and running creative strategy with Claude.

Rishi establishes why the topic matters by connecting creative strategy to Meta ads, which he describes as one of the largest and most important marketing channels available. His premise is that success on Meta can create meaningful company growth, especially for teams that are either just getting started or trying to improve an existing advertising system. He also gives context for his own authority: before joining The Rundown, he managed multiple seven figures in paid media for clients, and at The Rundown he works on a newsletter business with more than two million readers where Meta is the primary subscriber acquisition channel.

The core argument of the section is that creative strategy is the largest lever in Meta advertising because the creative itself influences where an ad is delivered and who responds to it. Rishi contrasts this with media buying, which he says has become easier for beginners because Meta has simplified the mechanics of launching campaigns. Rather than spending the session on campaign setup, he directs attention to the harder and more consequential question: whether the ads are actually good enough to win.

Rishi draws a firm boundary between outsourcing execution and outsourcing judgment. Production, editing, and other executional tasks can be handed off, but the strategic decisions behind creative should remain with the marketer because those decisions ultimately determine Meta ads performance. He positions the webinar as a way to strengthen that judgment while using Claude to move faster, not as a way to let AI replace the marketer's responsibility for deciding what to make, test, and scale.

He then previews the end-to-end workflow the session will cover: developing AI intuition, setting up a Claude project, building a core avatar, finding ad ideas through research, producing ads, naming concepts so performance data is readable, and analyzing results after ad spend. As the section closes, he begins defining where AI is useful and where caution is needed. AI is presented as strong for research, data consolidation, data analysis, ideation, hooks, angles, messaging, and copy polish, while the speaker warns that it should inform decisions rather than make them.
