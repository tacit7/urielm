# Setting Up Claude Projects and Brand Context

Time range: 07:10-17:20

## Synopsis

This section starts by drawing a practical boundary around how AI should be used in creative strategy. The speaker positions Claude as a powerful aid for research, data analysis, ideation, brainstorming hooks and angles, getting unstuck, and polishing copy before launch. At the same time, they warn against handing over the full creative process to AI. In their view, Claude can inform decisions, but the marketer still needs to supply judgment, taste, accountability, and the final call on what should actually ship.

The speaker is especially cautious about using AI to write all ad copy from scratch, make strategic decisions without oversight, or produce complete ads end-to-end with little human involvement. They argue that this often leads to average or below-average work, and that audiences can sense when ads lean too heavily on AI. The larger point is that Claude should accelerate and sharpen the creative process, not replace the human effort that makes an ad feel specific, intentional, and trustworthy.

From there, the section defines an ad "concept" as the combination of four variables: avatar, angle, offer, and format. The avatar is the person or audience being targeted; the angle is the argument the ad makes for why someone should pay attention or accept the offer; the offer is the packaged value exchange; and the format is the way the ad is visualized and delivered. The speaker emphasizes that these variables should be used to label ads and performance data so that, after spending money, the team can understand not only which ads worked but why they worked.

The speaker then shifts into the Claude project setup that will support the rest of the creative strategy workflow. The project is described as the central workspace where Claude receives the "golden context" it needs to help with ads in a useful way. The planned project contents include a core avatars file, a brand guidelines and company context file, project instructions, and a Meta Ads connector through the Meta MCP server. The speaker also recommends creating the Claude project from a local folder so the project files are stored both locally and in Claude.

The main setup walkthrough focuses on connecting Meta Ads and creating the brand context file. The Meta connector allows Claude to access ad account data directly instead of requiring manual exports and CSV uploads. The brand context file is built from a prompt that first asks for a company name and website, lets Claude research the business, and then has Claude interview the user with follow-up questions. The resulting document captures company positioning, products and offers, differentiation, conversion paths, funnel details, social proof, testimonials, objections, and other messaging context that helps Claude produce recommendations closer to what a knowledgeable marketer or team member would create.

## Key Details

- Claude is framed as useful for research, analysis, large-scale ideation, unblocking creative work, and editing copy before launch.
- The speaker cautions against using AI to write all copy from scratch, make final decisions without human oversight, or generate complete ads end-to-end.
- Human judgment remains central because Claude may lack full context, may not have better judgment, and cannot take responsibility for campaign outcomes.
- An ad concept is made up of four variables: avatar, angle, offer, and format.
- Concept labels make performance data more useful because they help explain why a creative asset performed, not just whether it performed.
- A Claude creative strategy project should include a core avatars file, a brand guidelines and company context file, project instructions, and a Meta Ads connection.
- The Meta Ads connector is added through Claude's connector flow by selecting the Meta Ads connector and logging into the relevant Meta account.
- The brand and company context file is generated through a prompt-driven process that combines Claude's web research with an interview-style set of follow-up questions.
- The brand context document should cover what the company does, its category and positioning, products and offers, unique mechanism or differentiation, conversion paths, marketing funnel, testimonials, objections, and messaging inputs.
- The section ends by teeing up the next major project asset: the core avatar, defined as the specific group of people most likely to buy and immediately recognize the ad as relevant to them.

## Why It Matters

- Clear boundaries for AI use help teams gain speed without letting generic AI output dilute ad quality.
- Preserving human judgment keeps creative strategy tied to accountability, taste, and real market understanding.
- Defining concepts through avatar, angle, offer, and format creates a repeatable system for planning tests and interpreting results.
- Labeling ads by concept variables turns campaign performance into learning that can guide future creative decisions.
- Centralizing brand, audience, instruction, and performance context gives Claude enough information to act like a useful strategy partner instead of a blank-chat copy generator.
- Connecting Meta Ads reduces manual data handling and makes it easier to bring performance evidence into the creative workflow.
- A detailed brand context file improves Claude's ability to produce messaging that reflects the company's actual product, positioning, proof, and customer objections.
