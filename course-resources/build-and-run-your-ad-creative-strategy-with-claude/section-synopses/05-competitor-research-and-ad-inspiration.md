# Competitor Research and Ad Inspiration

Time range: 31:25-47:55

## Synopsis

This section starts with the instructor deciding which of Claude's proposed avatar foundations should become the core audience for the ad strategy. Three options are on the table: someone seeking practical career skills rather than machine learning expertise, someone who already tried to learn AI through a course or tutorial and came away with nothing useful, and someone who uses AI daily but has private safeguards because they distrust the outputs. The instructor chooses the second option, later framed as the "burned learner," because it is emotionally specific, commercially useful, and easy to speak to in advertising.

The instructor explains why this avatar is stronger than the others. The burned learner has already shown desire, effort, and willingness to spend money, but they now carry skepticism because a prior AI learning experience failed to produce a usable work skill. That makes them a good fit for messaging built around a different mechanism: Rundown University can position live weekly office hours with an in-house expert as something the learner probably did not receive from the previous course. The instructor also connects this avatar to risk reversal, suggesting that a free trial or short refund window would directly answer the buyer's fear of wasting money again.

Once Claude builds the avatar document, the instructor emphasizes that it should be saved into project context and reviewed carefully because it becomes the foundation for later creative work. During the review, the instructor highlights the importance of "second-order benefits," meaning the deeper emotional outcomes behind surface-level claims. For example, the surface benefit might be learning AI tools for work, but the deeper benefit might be feeling capable, finishing a course instead of abandoning it, or looking smarter in front of a boss. The avatar file also introduces sub-avatar angles, such as the learner who is overwhelmed by scattered tutorials and wants one structured path rather than another content library.

The session then shifts from audience definition into ad inspiration. The instructor names four main sources of ad ideas: winning ads from competitors or adjacent brands, trending organic content, customer feedback and surveys, and a brand's own archive of winning ads. Because not every company has rich customer data or a large internal ad history, the demonstration focuses on a widely available source: Facebook Ad Library, which lets marketers inspect currently running Meta ads and use signals like volume, impressions, and longevity to infer what may be working.

For the live research example, the instructor shows how Claude and the Meta MCP can help identify relevant advertisers when the marketer does not already know where to look. At the same time, the instructor explains that useful inspiration does not need to come from a direct competitor. The example brand is Brilliant.org, which is not an AI education company but is adjacent because it sells educational content through a subscription model. The instructor sorts Brilliant's ads by impressions, looks for ads that have run for a long time, and treats those signals as a starting point for finding creative formats worth adapting to Rundown University.

## Key Details

- Claude proposes three possible avatar foundations, but the instructor chooses the "burned learner" because it combines prior intent, prior spending, disappointment, and renewed need.
- The chosen avatar is someone who already tried to learn AI through a course, certificate program, tutorial, or similar resource and did not gain a practical work skill.
- The instructor rejects the practical-career-skills avatar as too narrow for the intended advertising use, even though Claude surfaced data support for it.
- The skeptical daily-AI-user avatar is treated as a real but less immediately advertisable niche because the pain point is more unusual and harder to package.
- Rundown University's office hours become a unique mechanism that can differentiate the offer from static courses or passive content libraries.
- Risk reversal is presented as especially relevant for buyers who fear repeating a previous bad purchase.
- The avatar document should be saved into project context because later prompts and creative decisions will rely on it.
- "Second-order benefits" are framed as a key copywriting tool: marketers should ask why the buyer wants the surface benefit and then speak to that deeper desire.
- Sub-avatar work adds specificity to the core persona, such as targeting people who are tired of conflicting tutorials and want a single structured path.
- The instructor identifies four ad inspiration sources: competitor or adjacent-brand ads, trending organic content, customer feedback and surveys, and the brand's own winning ads.
- Facebook Ad Library is used because it exposes live Meta ads and can be searched by keyword, advertiser, or brand.
- Sorting by impressions and noticing long-running ads are treated as imperfect but useful indicators of ads that may be performing well.
- Brilliant.org is selected as an adjacent inspiration source because it shares an education and subscription model with Rundown University even though it teaches a different subject.
- The instructor cautions that competitor research is not usually a one-shot process; marketers may need to browse several pages before finding a useful ad format.

## Why It Matters

- Choosing the right avatar prevents the creative strategy from becoming too broad, too niche, or too detached from a buyer's real motivation.
- A strong avatar gives ad copy a believable emotional target, especially when the audience has a recognizable past frustration.
- Unique mechanisms and risk reversal help translate research insights into concrete offer angles rather than generic course messaging.
- Second-order benefits make ads more persuasive because they speak to the outcome buyers actually care about beneath the obvious feature.
- Adjacent-brand research expands the creative pool beyond direct competitors and helps marketers borrow proven formats without copying the exact niche.
- Facebook Ad Library provides a practical, repeatable way to find inspiration even when a marketer lacks internal performance data or extensive customer research.
