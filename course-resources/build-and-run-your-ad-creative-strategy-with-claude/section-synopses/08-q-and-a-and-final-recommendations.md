# Q&A and Final Recommendations

Time range: 71:45-end

## Synopsis

The closing section turns the training into a practical Q&A about how to run the Claude-assisted creative strategy system after the workshop. The presenter first points attendees back to the shared resource document and Rundown University materials, framing the prompts, documents, and examples as enough to run the strategy process end to end. The live questions then move from general workflow into specific implementation decisions: what data Claude needs, which model to use, how to keep testing manageable, and how to adapt the system for different audiences and creative formats.

The first major question asks how much Meta or TikTok ad-reporting data is needed to diagnose poor performance. The presenter explains that marketers can often start learning from the first $50 to $100 in spend, but the correct diagnosis depends on which part of the funnel is failing. He recommends breaking performance down sequentially: CPM shows how expensive it is to reach people, thumb-stop or hook metrics show whether the opening is getting attention, playthrough indicates whether the ad holds interest, click-through rate shows whether viewers are compelled to visit the page, and landing-page conversion rate shows whether the offer and page are converting traffic. The practical recommendation is to locate the first weak metric in the chain, then fix that part before assuming the entire ad or campaign is broken.

Several answers clarify where Claude fits relative to other tools. For Claude itself, the presenter says he currently uses Opus because his paid plan gives him enough usage, while Sonnet is a better choice for users who run out of tokens or need more volume from a lower-cost plan. He sees Claude as especially strong for copywriting and structured creative strategy, while ChatGPT is stronger for image generation; he also notes that newer ChatGPT models may change the comparison for copywriting over time. For research, he says he used Perplexity's computer/research workflow on a free plan and did not optimize heavily around the model choice, though he later noticed it appeared to be using a ChatGPT model.

The most strategic Q&A thread concerns testing design. When asked how to separate variables like hook, message, format, spokesperson, offer, and CTA without producing an unmanageable number of ads, the presenter gives a budget-driven answer: the larger the budget, the more variables can be tested; smaller budgets should test fewer ads with more attention paid to production quality. He recommends always creating multiple hooks for any ad concept because filming three or four openings usually adds very little cost or time. After hook testing, he would prioritize format and angle before expanding into larger matrices of offers, spokespersons, calls to action, and other variables.

The final recommendations address implementation edge cases and tool choices. For a 30-day creative testing plan, Claude needs the brand context file, core avatar research, available test budget, and desired outcome, but it still will not fully produce finished ads on its own. For non-English campaigns, the presenter suggests either using Meta's built-in AI translation features or prompting Claude to write ads directly in the target language, with the caveat that translation quality should be reviewed. For performance analysis, he returns to the importance of labeling ads by variables so Claude can identify which combinations are winning; as a rough spend threshold, he suggests judging an ad after about three to five times the target CPA. For Meta targeting, broad consumer products can often rely on creative to attract the right audience, while narrow B2B products may require uploading a prospect list built with a tool like Apollo. The session closes with recommendations for Atria for Instagram ad research, Gemini and ChatGPT for images, Claude Design for text-heavy images, and Higgsfield for short clips.

## Key Details

- The presenter says the shared Google document and Rundown University resources contain the prompts, documents, and examples needed to repeat the workflow after the session.
- Early campaign reads can begin with roughly $50 to $100 in spend, but the amount of data needed depends on the conversion goal and campaign economics.
- Poor performance should be diagnosed as a funnel problem rather than a single generic "bad ad" problem.
- CPM is the first diagnostic layer because high reach costs can indicate an expensive audience, weak engagement, negative reactions, or a platform reluctance to serve the ad.
- Thumb-stop metrics help diagnose whether the hook is strong enough to make people pause and watch.
- Playthrough, click-through rate, and landing-page conversion rate help separate creative retention, ad-to-page intent, and page or offer performance.
- The presenter currently prefers Claude Opus when usage limits allow, but recommends Claude Sonnet for users who need more token mileage.
- Claude is positioned as stronger for copywriting and creative strategy, while ChatGPT is recommended for image generation.
- Perplexity's research/computer workflow can be useful even on a free plan, though it may consume much of the available usage.
- Testing complexity should scale with budget: lower budgets should test fewer variables and put more care into each ad, while larger budgets can support broader variable matrices.
- The presenter recommends always testing multiple hooks because additional openings are cheap to produce when the creator is already filming the core ad.
- Format and angle are the next highest-priority variables after hooks.
- A 30-day testing plan should include brand context, core avatar research, available budget, and the target result the advertiser wants.
- For non-English audiences, marketers can either have Meta translate the ad during setup or prompt Claude to create the ad in the target language from the start.
- Claude can analyze winners and losers most effectively when ads are labeled by variables such as persona, angle, offer, and format.
- A rough decision threshold is three to five times the target CPA before deciding whether to scale, iterate, or kill an ad.
- Broad consumer campaigns may not need detailed Meta audience targeting because the creative itself can signal who should receive the ad.
- Highly specific B2B campaigns may need a custom uploaded audience built from prospect data, such as a list sourced through Apollo.
- Recommended supporting tools include Atria for Instagram ad research, Gemini and ChatGPT for image generation, Claude Design for text-heavy image concepts, and Higgsfield for short video clips.

## Why It Matters

- The Q&A translates the earlier framework into operational decisions marketers face when they actually launch tests.
- Diagnosing performance by funnel stage prevents teams from rewriting ads, changing offers, or rebuilding landing pages before they know which part is failing.
- Budget-based testing discipline keeps creative experimentation from turning into an unmanageable ad matrix.
- Prioritizing hooks, formats, and angles gives teams a practical testing order that balances learning value with production cost.
- Labeling creative variables turns ad reporting into strategy data, making it possible to see why something won instead of only seeing that it spent money.
- The language, targeting, and tool recommendations show how the workflow can adapt to different markets, platforms, and production constraints.
- The closing advice reinforces the central theme of the course: Claude can accelerate research, planning, analysis, and copy direction, but marketers still need to supply judgment, context, production choices, and performance interpretation.
