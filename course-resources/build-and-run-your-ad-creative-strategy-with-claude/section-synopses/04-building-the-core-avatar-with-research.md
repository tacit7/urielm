# Building the Core Avatar with Research

Time range: 17:20-31:25

## Synopsis

This section frames the core avatar as the first major audience-research asset in the creative strategy workflow. The instructor defines a core avatar as a specific group of people most likely to buy the product, with the goal that someone sees an ad and immediately thinks, "that's me." This matters most in crowded markets, where broad claims are already occupied by many competitors and where smaller budgets make it difficult to win by advertising generic category benefits.

The instructor argues that specificity creates a more defensible advertising angle. For Rundown University, a generic message like learning to use Claude or AI at work would compete against many creators, courses, and education platforms. A sharper avatar lets the brand speak to a narrower slice of the market by reflecting the audience's exact desires, prior experiences, emotions, and relevant demographics in the context of the product. The point is not to make the audience arbitrarily small, but to make the ad feel unusually relevant to the people most likely to care.

The research workflow begins in Perplexity rather than Claude because the instructor finds Perplexity more reliable for broad source scanning, especially across Reddit and other public web sources. The prompt asks for raw audience language rather than strategic conclusions, using sources such as Reddit threads, reviews, YouTube, Trustpilot, Coursera, and related websites. Reddit is emphasized because anonymous users often describe frustrations and desires more candidly, making their phrasing especially valuable for later ad copy and persona building.

After Perplexity produces the research document, the instructor treats it as raw data that should be reviewed rather than accepted uncritically. The example report scans hundreds of sources and organizes what people say, what they have been through, how they feel, and what they want from AI education. The instructor points out examples such as wanting practical AI use that maps to existing work, wanting AI to remove repetitive tasks, and wanting repeatable workflows or automation. These findings become the foundation for the Claude work that follows.

Before asking Claude to build the avatar, the instructor adds a separate core-avatar training document to the Claude project and asks Claude only to read it. This staged approach is meant to keep the human operator in control of the process. The training document teaches Claude the avatar framework, including the "specificity dial," the difference between core avatars and sub-avatars, and the danger of going too broad or too narrow. Only after Claude has this framework does the instructor use a phased build prompt: Claude first proposes multiple possible avatar foundations, then the human chooses the direction based on deeper business and customer judgment.

## Key Details

- A core avatar is defined as a specific group of likely buyers who should feel instant relatability when they see an ad.
- The instructor positions specificity as a way to compete in sophisticated markets where broad category promises are already crowded.
- A useful avatar combines desire, lived experiences, emotions, and relevant demographics, all tied to the offer being sold.
- The Rundown University example focuses on knowledge workers who want to use AI tools at work to improve productivity, capability, and efficiency.
- Perplexity is used for research because it can scan large numbers of sources and compile raw audience language quickly.
- The example research run reportedly scanned 939 sources, including Reddit, reviews, course platforms, YouTube, and other public web sources.
- Reddit is treated as especially valuable because anonymous users often describe their problems, doubts, and wants in unusually direct language.
- The research output is described as raw data, not a final strategic answer; the user should read it and check that it aligns with reality.
- Claude is trained with a separate core-avatar document before being asked to generate anything, so it understands the framework first.
- The phased Claude prompt asks for proposed avatar foundations before the final build, keeping the final strategic choice with the business owner.

## Why It Matters

- The avatar becomes the strategic foundation for later ad concepts, so weak or generic audience definition can weaken the entire creative process.
- Research-backed language helps ads sound closer to how the audience actually talks, rather than how marketers assume they talk.
- Separating raw research from strategic judgment reduces the risk of letting AI invent a direction without enough grounding.
- Training Claude before prompting it helps align the model with the intended framework and makes the output more useful.
- Requiring the human to choose among avatar options preserves business judgment, customer knowledge, and accountability in the workflow.
