# Introduction to AI Consulting

Time range: 00:00-04:08

## Synopsis

This opening section frames the course around a practical question: how to land a first AI consulting gig and begin making money with AI. The speaker positions AI consulting as the main path under discussion, with the goal of helping viewers decide whether consulting is a good fit for them and understand a concrete zero-to-one strategy for getting started. The emphasis is not on an abstract overview of the AI market, but on actionable guidance for moving from curiosity or early experimentation into paid client work.

The speaker also sets expectations for the session format. Rather than spending the entire hour on slides, they plan to use only part of the time for presentation and devote a significant portion to practical demonstration. They intend to walk through what they would do if starting from scratch, including a live attempt to create a reusable skill or process aid that can support learners as they pursue their own consulting work.

The personal background establishes credibility while also making the path feel accessible. The speaker trained as an economist, worked for six years as a machine learning engineer at Zelle, and then moved into AI consulting. They acknowledge that their technical and professional background helped, but they explicitly state that AI-specific experience is not required to start earning meaningful money in this field. This distinction is important: prior experience can be an advantage, but the course is framed for people who may be starting with far less formal AI expertise.

The speaker explains that they left a job that looked ideal on paper: remote, flexible, and not especially demanding. The source of dissatisfaction was that the job did not allow them to use AI, which mattered because they had become deeply interested in AI tools and wanted to work with them directly. Their own transition was not entirely risk-free; they left before consulting fully replaced their income. Still, they advise others to be more measured when possible, especially if they have a full-time job: build consulting work on the side, get money coming in first, and consider waiting until income is at or near replacement level before making a full leap.

The section closes this introductory arc with the speaker's AI timeline. They began using ChatGPT in early 2024, moved into daily Cursor usage by mid-2024 for side projects and startup work, adopted Claude Code as soon as it became available, and later went deep on Codex. By October, they had started getting consulting clients through their network, which eventually led to a client hiring them into a role. This progression shows how experimentation with tools, side projects, network-driven opportunities, and early client work can compound into a more specialized AI consulting practice.

- The course's stated goal is to help learners understand how to make money with AI through consulting.
- The speaker promises both fit assessment and concrete strategy: deciding whether AI consulting is right for the learner and how to go from zero to one.
- The session is designed to be practical, with less emphasis on slides and more emphasis on walking through what the speaker would do from scratch.
- The speaker's background includes economics training, six years as a machine learning engineer at Zelle, and later independent AI consulting.
- Prior AI-specific experience is presented as helpful but not mandatory for getting started or earning decent money.
- The speaker's dissatisfaction with their previous job centered on not being allowed to use AI, despite the job being remote, flexible, and manageable.
- Their transition included some risk: they left before consulting fully replaced their salary.
- The recommended safer path is to start consulting on the side, especially for those with full-time jobs, and wait until there is real income before making a bigger leap.
- The speaker's tool progression included ChatGPT, Cursor, Claude Code, and Codex, with each stage contributing to deeper AI practice.
- Early consulting clients came through the speaker's network and helped create momentum toward a more specialized consulting practice.

## Why It Matters

- It defines AI consulting as a practical income path rather than merely a technical specialty.
- It lowers the barrier to entry by separating useful prior experience from strict prerequisites.
- It gives learners a realistic model for risk management: experiment, moonlight, find clients, and avoid quitting too early without revenue.
- It shows that tool fluency can develop through side projects and consistent use, not only through formal AI roles.
- It frames the rest of the course around implementation and client value, preparing learners to think about consulting as paid problem-solving.
