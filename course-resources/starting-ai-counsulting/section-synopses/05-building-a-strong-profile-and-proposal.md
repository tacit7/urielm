# Building a Strong Profile and Proposal

Time range: 35:48-47:58

## Synopsis

This section explains how beginners should use Upwork job postings not only as opportunities to apply for paid work, but also as a practical guide for what to learn next. The instructor cautions against immediately applying to every appealing post. Instead, a posting should be treated as proof that someone is willing to pay for a specific capability, such as scraping data, using Git, connecting API calls to AI tools, building dashboards, or producing end reports. If a learner does not yet understand the tools or workflow named in the job post, that gap becomes the next project to build.

The instructor frames project-based learning as the bridge between skill development and profile credibility. A learner can take the requirements from a real job post, attempt an analogous project, and use the result as a portfolio piece. Even if the project was not completed for a real client, it can still demonstrate practical ability when presented honestly as a built example. The instructor recommends packaging these projects with short Loom videos that walk through what was built, ideally in one or two minutes, so potential clients can quickly see evidence of competence.

The section then connects proposal strategy with profile strength. A strong proposal can get a client to click, especially if the opening sentence acts as a specific hook, such as mentioning a sample already scraped or a quick Loom prepared for the client. But once the client clicks through, the profile has to carry the next step. A well-structured profile with relevant keywords, clear positioning, project videos, and proof-like examples makes it more likely that the client will respond rather than dismiss the freelancer as another generic applicant.

Using his own Upwork profile as a reference point, the instructor describes the kinds of signals beginners should create. He emphasizes matching the profile headline and description to the market being targeted, such as data scraping, Python, or TypeScript, rather than using vague generalist language. He also suggests that beginners keep their hourly rate relatively low at first because the early goal is not maximizing rate, but getting initial jobs, reviews, and relationships. Those early relationships can later lead to larger fixed-price work or repeat client opportunities.

The instructor also highlights video as a differentiator. An introduction video helps a freelancer seem more credible because most applicants will not have one. Project walkthrough videos add further evidence, especially when they show relevant work tied to the kinds of jobs being pursued. The broader lesson is that beginners should build a profile around the exact work they want to win: study job posts, build targeted examples, show those examples clearly, and use the profile as an evidence-rich landing page for every proposal.

## Key Details

- Upwork job posts can be used as market research for what clients are actively paying for.
- Beginners should pause before applying and ask whether they can actually complete the tasks described in the post.
- Missing skills from real postings should become the next project-based learning assignment.
- Portfolio pieces do not have to come from real clients; they can be self-directed projects modeled on real client needs.
- Short Loom videos are recommended for profile projects because they quickly demonstrate what was built and how it works.
- A proposal hook should be specific enough to make the client want to click, especially by referencing a relevant sample or quick demonstration.
- Once a client clicks the proposal, the profile needs to reinforce trust through structure, keywords, videos, and evidence of ability.
- Beginners should position themselves around concrete services, such as data scraping, rather than broad AI consulting language.
- The instructor recommends using relevant technical keywords like Python, TypeScript, Git, and scraping when they match the work being pursued.
- Early Upwork work may be underpriced strategically in order to earn reviews, build credibility, and form client relationships.
- Introductory profile videos and short project walkthroughs can help a beginner stand out because most applicants will not provide that level of proof.
- The profile should evolve from the learner's actual projects, and those projects should be chosen based on the kinds of jobs they want to win.

## Why It Matters

- A beginner with no network or reputation needs visible proof of skill before clients will take their proposals seriously.
- Real job posts reveal the market's current demand more clearly than abstract study plans.
- Turning job requirements into learning projects helps students avoid unfocused practice and build assets they can reuse in sales.
- Proposal writing and profile building work together: the proposal earns the click, while the profile earns the response.
- Videos, specific examples, and relevant keywords reduce the perceived risk of hiring a new freelancer.
- Early jobs are valuable not only for money, but for reviews, relationships, and momentum on the platform.
