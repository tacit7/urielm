# Learning AI Through Projects

Time range: 04:08-11:43

## Synopsis

The speaker frames AI consulting as consulting first and AI second. The core job is not to show off tools, but to solve concrete business problems well enough that the value is obvious to the client. If a problem is costing a business tens of thousands of dollars, then a consultant who can solve or meaningfully reduce that problem has a legitimate basis for charging a meaningful fee. In this framing, AI tools such as Claude Code, Codex, and large language models are practical instruments for delivering outcomes, not the whole identity of the work.

From there, the speaker breaks the learning path into two parallel tracks: learning how to consult and learning how to use AI effectively. Consulting includes problem discovery, stakeholder communication, sales, pricing, and implementation judgment. AI competence includes knowing how to use agentic coding tools, how to reason with them, and how to explain their value to nontechnical stakeholders. The speaker is explicit that both tracks require continuous improvement; even experienced practitioners keep studying areas like enterprise sales, client acquisition, and new AI workflows.

The main recommendation for learning AI is to learn through projects rather than through abstract study alone. For someone starting from zero, the suggested first move is to get an affordable Codex subscription and begin building small personal projects, such as a personal website. Once that baseline project works, the learner should deliberately extend it with features that are slightly beyond their current ability: authentication, interactivity, a mobile app version, or another improvement that forces them to stretch. Each project should teach only the minimum concepts needed to finish the next useful thing.

The speaker emphasizes that growth requires regularly moving beyond what feels comfortable. Repeating the same simple project may build confidence, but it eventually becomes stale and limits learning. Instead, the learner should choose projects that are just outside their current range, accepting that confusion and discomfort are normal signs of progress. This habit matters for both AI and consulting because client work rarely arrives as a perfectly familiar exercise; it usually requires learning, adapting, and explaining unfamiliar ideas under practical constraints.

Finally, the speaker recommends using AI itself as a learning partner. When concepts such as Git, worktrees, skills, or sub-agents are unclear, learners can ask Codex, Claude Code, ChatGPT, or Claude to explain them. However, the speaker warns against passively accepting explanations. The learner should interrogate the answer, restate the concept in their own words, ask the AI to test their understanding, and invite it to identify weaknesses or inaccuracies. The goal is active understanding, not merely consuming polished explanations.

## Key Details

- AI consulting is presented as problem-solving for businesses, with AI tools serving the consulting outcome.
- The speaker narrows the operational focus to using Claude Code, Codex, and large language models to solve client problems.
- Learners need to improve in both consulting skills and AI skills, since client value depends on both delivery and communication.
- The recommended starting point is a low-cost Codex subscription and simple personal projects.
- A personal website is suggested as an approachable first project before adding harder features.
- Good learning projects should be slightly outside the learner's current range.
- Discomfort is described as a normal and necessary part of improving.
- AI tools can help explain unfamiliar AI and development concepts, but learners should actively challenge and test the explanations.
- The speaker suggests asking AI to quiz the learner or critique the learner's own explanation of a concept.

## Why It Matters

- This section gives beginners a practical path into AI consulting without requiring them to master everything first.
- It shifts the focus from credentials or theoretical knowledge to demonstrated ability through completed projects.
- It connects learning directly to future client work, where the consultant must solve messy, valuable problems.
- It encourages a sustainable learning loop: build, stretch, get stuck, ask better questions, and deepen understanding.
- It warns against passive course consumption and promotes active practice as the route to real competence.
